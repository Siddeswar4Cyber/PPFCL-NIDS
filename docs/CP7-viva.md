# CP7 viva notes

**What is implemented?** Twelve C1 ownership manifests: two datasets, two protocols and three requested allocation scenarios. Each manifest assigns the same 100,000 training representatives and its full validation matrix to five logical clients. H1 separately evaluates four fixed host-separation candidates. No FL/model training occurs in this checkpoint.

**Why can we not simply split NF by host?** Every attack in each permitted development population belongs to one connected endpoint component. A flow connects both endpoints, and feature groups can connect components as well. Keeping all flows and preventing shared endpoints means splitting between components. One attack component cannot supply attacks to both separated sides. This is a conditional statement about the observed population, not every possible future dataset.

**What happened when cross-side groups were removed?** The fixed endpoint-filter design retained 1,208 primary validation rows, but only two were benign. Stress retained 439, with one benign. These fail the preregistered support floors. We preserved the candidate ledgers and rejection reasons, did not change seeds to hunt for support, and did not score a classifier on those candidates.

**Are the five clients five banks or independent hosts?** No. They are simulated ownership partitions of flow groups. Hosts can occur in multiple logical clients. This supports comparison under controlled label/quantity heterogeneity, with no claim of institutional realism or independent-host generalization.

**How is group leakage avoided?** Ownership is a deterministic function of the S1 group, scope and training-derived label probabilities. Every repeated validation vector has the same owner. Training and validation retain the original S1 roles, and the verifier checks zero shared groups across roles or owners. No identifiers or labels become detector features.

**Is the alpha-0.1 scenario always pure Dirichlet?** No. Three scopes exhausted 20 attempts below the training-support floors and used the registered 10% uniform mixture. NF primary accepted its tenth pure draw. Reports distinguish the actual methods and preserve rejected attempts. Accepted distributions are conditioned on minimum support, so do not describe them as unconstrained random Dirichlet draws or assume their realized skew is strictly ordered by alpha.

**Does using validation labels for ownership select the partition?** The partition rule explicitly simulates label skew. All probabilities and the acceptance decision come from training only. The fixed rule then assigns validation groups using their labels, with a uniform fallback for labels absent in training. It is label-aware simulated evaluation ownership, not naturally observed client traffic. Validation support does not trigger retries.

**What is the preprocessing privacy boundary?** The common P1/P2 transforms were fitted centrally on permitted development samples. C1 is a non-private simulation contract. Private allocation, private/public preprocessing, DP sampling and release accounting, and CL-specific client/task scopes still require separate review and artifacts.

**Can we continue if host separation failed?** Yes, with a narrower claim. The verified C1 manifests support comparable centralized, local and FL development experiments. CP8 must first verify neural-framework migration, numeric behavior and optimizer stability. The project must keep host/institution generalization and privacy claims separate from those simulations.
